PASSAGE 1
The Amazon rainforest, often referred to as the "lungs of the Earth," covers approximately 5.5 million square kilometers across nine nations. Brazil contains 60% of the rainforest, followed by Peru with 13%, and Colombia with 10%. It produces about 20% of the world's oxygen and houses at least 10% of the world's known biodiversity. In 2019, satellite data from the National Institute for Space Research (INPE) revealed an 85% increase in fires compared to the previous year. Most of these fires were deliberately set by ranchers and loggers seeking to clear land for cattle grazing and soybean cultivation. The resulting deforestation not only destroys habitats but also releases massive amounts of stored carbon back into the atmosphere, significantly accelerating global climate change.

(B) Colombia

(D) Venezuela

Answer: C
Explanation: The passage directly states: "Brazil contains 60% of the rainforest..."

(C) 20%
(D) 60%

Answer: C
Explanation: The text explicitly mentions: "It produces about 20% of the world's oxygen..."

(B) 13%
(C) 60%
(D) 85%

Answer: D
Explanation: The passage explicitly states: "satellite data... revealed an 85% increase in fires compared to the previous year."

PASSAGE 2
The Great Wall of China is not a single, continuous wall but a series of fortifications built across the historical northern borders of ancient Chinese states and Imperial China. Construction of the earliest walls began as early as the 7th century BC. However, the most famous and best-preserved sections were built during the Ming Dynasty (1368–1644 AD). Measuring approximately 21,196 kilometers in total length, it was designed primarily to protect against nomadic intrusions from the Eurasian Steppe. Apart from defense, the wall served as a means of border control, allowing the imposition of duties on goods transported along the Silk Road, and the regulation of trade and immigration.

PASSAGE 3
Marie Curie was a Polish and naturalized-French physicist and chemist who conducted pioneering research on radioactivity. She was the first woman to win a Nobel Prize, the first person to win a Nobel Prize twice, and the only person to win a Nobel Prize in two different scientific fields. She won her first Nobel Prize in Physics in 1903, sharing it with her husband Pierre Curie and physicist Henri Becquerel for their work on spontaneous radiation. In 1911, she was awarded the Nobel Prize in Chemistry for her discovery of the elements polonium and radium. During World War I, Curie developed mobile radiography units to provide X-ray services to field hospitals. Tragically, her decades of unprotected exposure to radiation ultimately caused her death from aplastic anemia in 1934.

Explanation: The text states she is "the only person to win a Nobel Prize in two different scientific fields."
