----------------------------------------
Topic: Phrase Replacement
Subtopic: Grammar Based
----------------------------------------

Q1. He is **one of the best player** in the entire tournament this year.
(A) one of the best players
(B) one of best player
(C) the best of player
(D) No replacement required

Answer: A
Explanation: The phrase "one of" is always followed by a plural noun. Therefore, "player" must be pluralized to "players".

Q2. If I **would have known** about the traffic, I would have left earlier.
(A) had known
(B) will have known
(C) have known
(D) No replacement required

Answer: A
Explanation: In a third conditional sentence (unreal past), the "if" clause must use the past perfect tense ("had known"). "Would have" is only used in the main clause.

Q3. The jury **were divided in its** opinion regarding the final verdict.
(A) was divided in its
(B) were divided in their
(C) was divided in their
(D) No replacement required

Answer: B
Explanation: The collective noun "jury" takes a plural verb ("were") and a plural pronoun ("their") when the members are acting individually or disagreeing. 

Q4. Scarcely had she finished reading the book **than the phone rang**.
(A) then the phone rang
(B) when the phone rang
(C) while the phone rang
(D) No replacement required

Answer: B
Explanation: The correlative conjunction "scarcely" is always followed by "when", not "than".

Q5. Neither the manager nor the employees **is willing to compromise** on the new policy.
(A) are willing to compromise
(B) has willing to compromise
(C) was willing to compromise
(D) No replacement required

Answer: A
Explanation: In a "neither...nor" construction, the verb must agree with the subject closest to it. "Employees" is plural, so the verb should be "are".

Q6. The new software is highly superior **than the older version** we were using last year.
(A) than the old version
(B) over the older version
(C) to the older version
(D) No replacement required

Answer: C
Explanation: Adjectives ending in "-ior" (such as superior, inferior, senior, junior) are followed by the preposition "to", not the conjunction "than".

Q7. Despite of **his rigorous training**, he failed to qualify for the finals.
(A) Despite his rigorous training
(B) In spite his rigorous training
(C) Despite with his rigorous training
(D) No replacement required

Answer: A
Explanation: The word "despite" takes no preposition. "In spite of" is the correct alternative, but "despite of" is grammatically incorrect.

Q8. She prefers reading classical literature **than to watch** contemporary movies.
(A) rather than watch
(B) to watching
(C) than watching
(D) No replacement required

Answer: B
Explanation: When using the verb "prefer" to compare two gerunds or nouns, the preposition "to" must be used. Both elements being compared must be gerunds: "reading... to watching".

Q9. Hardly I had stepped out of the house **when it started** raining heavily.
(A) Hardly had I stepped out of the house
(B) Hardly I stepped out of the house
(C) Hard had I stepped out of the house
(D) No replacement required

Answer: A
Explanation: When a sentence begins with a negative adverb like "Hardly," the subject and auxiliary verb must be inverted. It should be "Hardly had I".

Q10. It is high time that the government **takes strict action** against tax evaders.
(A) took strict action
(B) take strict action
(C) will take strict action
(D) No replacement required

Answer: A
Explanation: The phrase "It is high time" is followed by a clause in the simple past tense to indicate something that should have been done already. "Takes" should be "took".

Q11. The committee has discussed the matter at length, but **have not reached** a conclusion yet.
(A) has not reached
(B) having not reached
(C) had not reached
(D) No replacement required

Answer: A
Explanation: The collective noun "committee" acts as a single unit here, as shown by the first verb "has". The second verb must maintain parallel subject-verb agreement. Therefore, "have" should be "has".

Q12. I look forward **to meet you** at the annual conference next month.
(A) to meeting you
(B) for meeting you
(C) to have met you
(D) No replacement required

Answer: A
Explanation: The phrase "look forward to" ends with a preposition ("to"), which must be followed by a noun or a gerund (-ing form). Therefore, "meet" must be "meeting".

Q13. Not only did he break the law, **but he also destroys** the evidence.
(A) but he also destroyed
(B) but he destroys also
(C) and he also destroyed
(D) No replacement required

Answer: A
Explanation: To maintain parallel tense structure, since the first clause is in the past tense ("did he break"), the second clause must also be in the past tense ("destroyed").

Q14. The reason why he was rejected from the job **was because he lacked** the necessary communication skills.
(A) is because he lacked
(B) was that he lacked
(C) was due to he lacked
(D) No replacement required

Answer: B
Explanation: The phrase "The reason why" implies cause, making the word "because" redundant. It should be followed by a "that" clause.

15. By the time the doctor arrives, the patient **will die**.
(A) will have died
(B) would die
(C) is going to die
(D) No replacement required

Answer: A
Explanation: To describe an action that will be completed before another action in the future (the doctor arriving), the future perfect tense ("will have died") must be used.
