Passage 2 (Questions 6-10):
Marine biology studies have recently uncovered the extent of microplastic pollution in the deepest parts of the ocean. In a 2023 expedition to the Mariana Trench, researchers collected sediment samples from a depth of 10,900 meters. The analysis revealed an average of 45 microplastic particles per liter of sediment, significantly higher than surface water concentrations. These particles predominantly consist of polyethylene terephthalate (PET) and nylon. The primary source of this pollution is terrestrial runoff, combined with the gradual breakdown of discarded fishing gear, often referred to as "ghost nets". Marine organisms at these depths, such as amphipods, have been found to ingest these plastics, which can lead to intestinal blockages and a false sense of satiation, ultimately causing starvation.

Passage 3 (Questions 11-15):
The history of the printing press is a cornerstone of modern civilization. Invented by Johannes Gutenberg in Mainz, Germany, around 1440, the press introduced movable type to Europe. Prior to this, books were painstakingly copied by hand by scribes, primarily in monasteries, making them rare and expensive. Gutenberg's first major publication was the 'Forty-Two-Line Bible' in 1455. The technology spread rapidly; by 1500, printing presses were operating throughout Western Europe and had already produced more than twenty million volumes. This era, known as the incunabula period, democratized knowledge, facilitating the Renaissance, the Scientific Revolution, and the Protestant Reformation. However, the exact alloy Gutenberg used for his typepieces—a mix of lead, tin, and antimony—was a closely guarded secret that allowed the metal to melt at low temperatures and cast well in the die.
